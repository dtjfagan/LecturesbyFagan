## The Charlotte Super Fab Lab

A Fab Lab is a digital fabrication workshop where you can take an idea from a sketch to a working part. A **Super Fab Lab** is a step above: a designation from the nonprofit Fab Foundation for labs that can build much of their own equipment and support other labs. UNC Charlotte opened its Super Fab Lab in August 2024 and became the **first public university in the United States** to earn the designation. Only a handful exist worldwide.

The lab includes:

- CNC milling and a water jet cutter
- Precision metrology (measuring parts accurately)
- Circuit board production
- A robotic arm
- Entry-level and professional-grade machines, side by side

It's a partnership between the Lee College of Engineering and the College of Computing and Informatics, and it also serves the Charlotte region's 3,600-plus manufacturers. Local companies and small businesses can use equipment that is usually only available to large organizations.

What I want you to take from it: "With the Super Fab Lab, our students can be more agile than industry. It puts the power in the students' hands so they can reach a final product much faster than traditional methods."

## What I care about

- **Hands-on learning.** You learn engineering by building, testing, breaking, and rebuilding, not only by reading about it.
- **From maker to manufacturer.** I think a lot about the path from a one-off prototype in a lab to a product made at scale, and about what that path means for U.S. manufacturing.
- **Safety and ownership.** A good lab is one where everyone, including the newest student, feels able to speak up.
- **Honest engineering.** Know what you can defend, and say clearly what you don't know yet.

## When engineering meets a crisis

In spring 2020, at the start of the COVID-19 pandemic, hospitals were short on protective equipment. I helped lead a UNC Charlotte effort to make face shields. We started with 3D printers, at about 100 shields a day, then worked with industrial partners to move to mass manufacturing. Together we produced more than **100,000 face shields**.

That project is a good example of what I hope you learn here: the same skills that make a prototype can, with the right partners, make a difference at scale.
